PhyloDiamond.jl

PhyloDiamond.jl is a Julia package to find where a hybridization happened, from gene trees or concordance factors. It looks for a phylogenetic network with one 4-node hybridization cycle, and uses algebraic invariants instead of likelihood. This makes it very fast: on the Canis data in the paper below, it took 7 seconds, compared with 2 minutes for SNaQ.

When to use it

Use PhyloDiamond to quickly find which taxa are involved in a hybridization: the hybrid clade and its two parent clades. It is a good first step: it narrows thousands of candidate networks down to a few. You can then compare those with a likelihood method such as SNaQ, which also estimates inheritance probabilities and branch lengths.

PhyloDiamond needs at least 6 taxa and assumes a single 4-node hybridization cycle. See The method in brief for what it can and cannot estimate.

Citation

If you use PhyloDiamond.jl in your work, we kindly ask that you cite the following paper:

  • Wu, Z., Solís-Lemus, C. (2024). Ultrafast learning of four-node hybridization cycles in phylogenetic networks using algebraic invariants. Bioinformatics Advances, 4(1), vbae014. doi:10.1093/bioadv/vbae014.
